v8.00 Results: v7.08 refit on the Nadir recordings (the baseline v8 has to beat)
================================================================================

Date: 2026-10-17
Scripts: backend/scripts/experiments/exp_v8_00/nadir_export.py      (loader + QC + inventory)
         backend/scripts/experiments/exp_v8_00/exp_v8_00_refit.py   (per-session v7.08 refit)
Outputs: backend/scripts/experiments/exp_v8_00/results/<group>.json and <group>.png

Nothing about the model changes in this experiment. The v7.08 Stage A and Stage B fit
functions are imported from exp_v7_08_ship.py and run, with the shipping configuration
and seed, on each session that has SpO2. The question is only: do v7's parameters hold
across sessions and devices, or was v7 a curve fit to 21 February 2026?

Loader decisions, all forced by v7's code rather than chosen:

  - v7's Stage B convolves the trace with the gamma kernel as an array, so it needs a
    complete 1 Hz grid. Dropouts and samples without skin contact are filled by linear
    interpolation. Every long gap in the app-recorded holds (11 to 21 s) sits in the first
    25 s of the hold on the 98-100 % plateau, where interpolation changes nothing. The
    one hold where the gap crosses the descent (18 September, 102 s without contact) is
    excluded.
  - Recovery follows v7's rule: up to 90 s after the hold, stopping once SpO2 is back at
    97 %. Nadir stores vitals per step, so the loader walks the steps after the hold.
  - QC is v7's: range < 5 % excluded; a nadir more than 30 s before the end of the hold
    excluded (v7 dropped RV#4 at 67 s for this reason and kept FRC#2 at 12 s). Two
    coverage rules were added for the Nadir data: more than 20 % of samples filled, or a
    gap longer than 10 s below 95 %, excludes the hold. v7 itself trained on FL#6 with a
    6 s gap near its nadir, so the line is above that.
  - Nadir `full` maps to v7 FL, `rv` to RV. There are no FRC holds. `packed` is skipped:
    v7 has no bounds for it. One session's labels are overridden from Thomas's answer
    (6 August: RV x8, then full); the inventory and every fit below use the corrected ones.

Hold windows differ slightly from v7's own: the CSV's Apnea interval for FRC#2 is 135 s
where the v7 database had 132, because the app's parser trimmed rows the loader keeps.
The February refit below is therefore a close reproduction of v7.08, not a bit-identical
one, and that is the point: the same code on the same session through a second loader.


2. THINGS THE DATA SAYS BEFORE ANY FITTING
------------------------------------------

  - The 6 August "full" holds were RV holds. Eight holds of 65 to 138 s reach 58 % SpO2,
    which is RV behaviour (the 16 October RV table reaches 42 % at 159 s; the September
    full-lung holds need 300 s to reach 80 %). The label was the STAmina importer's
    default. Read from the traces before asking: holds 1 to 8 drop below 95 % at 66 to
    74 s into the hold, where the PM100 RV tables do at 59 to 72 s and February's two FRC
    holds at 106 s; hold 9 (321 s, below 95 % at 200 s, nadir 79 %) matches the 8 August
    full-lung holds. Thomas confirmed exactly that. The onset of desaturation is a usable
    lung-volume check on any imported session, and the mislabelled fit (section 4, item 7)
    shows what v7 does when the type bounds are wrong.
  - The 2 October session ("True tidal breathing") alternates an RV hold with a full-lung
    hold seven seconds later, with no recovery between them. The full-lung holds start at
    79 to 89 % with the reading still falling from the RV hold before, so their apnea-
    window minimum is at the start and v7's QC drops all five ("nadir N s before end").
    That is the right outcome for a model that assumes a settled baseline, and a protocol
    v8 would have to represent explicitly (initial state carried over from the last hold).
  - The post-hold window is short. Nadir's recovery breathe step was 20 s (now 6 s), and
    after the last hold of a session the trace ends with it. On 4 and 5 September the
    max holds end with SpO2 still falling (76 % and 71 % at the last sample); on 12 October
    the 345 s full-lung hold has 7 s of trace after it. The nadir of the longest holds is
    not in the data. Where a calm-breathe step follows the recovery step, the loader's
    stitching recovers 30 to 60 s, which is why the RV tables fare better.
  - Lag is visibly different per hold on the same device: nadir 1 s after the end of
    2026-09-05-FL1, 10 s after FL2 the same evening, 29 s after 2026-09-02-FL1. v7's
    per-hold delta_h existed for this; the spread here (-16 to +20 s, see below) is far
    wider than February's (-8 to +7 s).
  - Readings are integers that sit flat for seconds at a time on both devices (median run
    3 to 4 s; a fifth of runs last 10 s or more) and now and then freeze and jump. In holds
    the freeze-then-jump rate (a run of 8 s or more followed by a step of 3 points or more)
    is the same on both devices, 1.0 per 1000 s; the PM100's extra freezes are in activity
    recordings and mostly coincide with contact loss. The device difference that survives
    the counting is slower: the reading's first rise after a hold comes a median 37 s after
    the end on the PM100 against 29 s on the Masimo, and the PM100's nadir delay spreads
    1 to 47 s against 10 to 35 s. exp_v8_01 takes this up; a paired recording is what
    would separate device from finger, movement and sampling.
  - The 8 September session is a CO2 table (five 150 s holds). SpO2 barely moves in four
    of them and v7's QC drops them. CO2 tables are not desaturation data.
  - RV is now the best-covered lung volume: 18 app-recorded RV holds in four sessions, 60
    to 159 s, nadirs 42 to 93 %. Full-lung has 8 app-recorded holds, most of them 300 s
    or longer with the nadir cut off. No session has FRC holds. v7's FRC bound was its
    load-bearing constraint; there is nothing here to test it against.
  - Six traces go below 50 % and 16 below 70 %, the floor of the range finger
    oximeters are validated for. They are kept and flagged in the inventory by their
    nadir; nothing below reads them as physiology.

4. WHAT THE REFIT SHOWS
-----------------------

  1. The pipeline reproduces v7.08. On the February session the new loader gives tau_0
     17.68 (v7.08: 17.68), p 3.40 (3.39), deltas within 0.03 s of the published ones,
     gamma 1.85 (1.86), the same three bound hits and the same +17 s nadir miss on FRC#2.
     k_co2 lands at 0.151 instead of 0.139 and FL6's tau_washout at 408 instead of 377
     (RV3's at 52 instead of 49.5): the two moved together, which is the k_co2 vs washout
     trade-off the spec documented in its own profile. The likely cause is the window:
     the loader keeps 3 to 7 apnea seconds per hold that the app's parser trimmed (FRC2
     +3, RV3 +4, FRC5 +3, FL6 +7). An 8 % shift in k_co2 from a few seconds of window is
     itself a finding about k_co2. Everything below is therefore about the data, not the
     code.

  2. The globals drift. Refitted per session, tau_0 is 6.6 to 39.1 s (17.7 in February),
     k_co2 sits at its 0.02 lower bound in four of nine Nadir sessions and at 0.145 in
     another, gamma runs 1.16 to 2.51. p hits its upper bound once. None of this is
     within the spread v7 called "identified".

  3. The globals do not matter. Freezing all four at February's values and refitting only
     the per-hold parameters changes the apnea-window R2a by less than 0.025 on every
     app-recorded session with real desaturation (table 3.2). The per-hold tau_washout
     absorbs the difference: RV washout goes 42 to 80 s (the bound) on 2 October and
     full-lung 321 to 500 s (the bound) on 4 September to make room for February's faster
     CO2 rise. The 6 August Masimo session behaves the same way (-0.003); under its
     original wrong full-lung labels it had lost 0.15 with every paco2_0 at a bound, so
     the fit was sensitive to the labels, never to the globals. Items 2 and 3 together
     are the diagnosis: the globals wander because the data cannot pin
     them, and the data cannot pin them because every hold has two free knobs of its own.
     v7's k_co2 profile had an interior minimum only because the FRC bound held the FRC
     holds' tau_washout at 100 s. There are no FRC holds here, and nothing else holds it.

  4. The sensor lag is not a lag. Per-hold deltas sat within -8 to +7 s in February; here
     they sit at or within 0.1 s of the +20 s bound on the longest full-lung hold of three
     sessions (effective lag 46 to 51 s) and two holds sit at the 5 s floor. Those three
     holds have 0 to 20 s of trace after them, so the nadir-timing term has nothing to
     grip and the kernel is set by the integer plateaus of a slow descent instead. The
     traces are piecewise constant on both devices (results/2026-10-16.png shows it on six
     RV holds in a row), and v7's gamma kernel can only smooth, never hold and step; but the
     counting in exp_v8_01 says the plateau-and-step pattern inside holds is the same on the
     Masimo and the PM100, so it is a property of integer oximetry of a slow signal, not of
     one device. What is device-specific is the slower recovery onset on the PM100 (37 s
     against 29 s median); with one device per period that cannot yet be separated from
     month, finger or training phase.

  5. Stage A is fragile. With two holds per session it put 2026-09-05-FL1 at S_min 97 %,
     v_up 2.8 %/s and lag at the floor (R2a -0.05) while Stage B fits the same hold at
     0.94; v_up is at its 3 %/s bound on five holds in three sessions. On the RV
     tables the latent template misses the staircase (2026-10-16-RV2: Stage A 0.57, Stage
     B 0.92). Eight to twenty parameters against 2 to 8 holds with 5 to 60 s of recovery
     is not identified, and the nadir-timing penalty has nothing to grip when the nadir
     is not in the trace.

  6. Stage B still describes the apnea window, as a curve fit does. Sessions whose holds
     reach below 85 % fit at R2a 0.90 to 0.96 with per-hold tau_washout and paco2_0 free.
     RV washout by session: 42, 45, 48, 50, 77 s (February 52); full-lung: 191, 212,
     243, 321, 381 s (February 408). paco2_0 sits at a bound on seven holds in six sessions, at
     both ends (30 at the bottom, 50 and 55 at the top). Nothing here is a parameter
     you could carry into a hold you have not seen.

  7. Labels matter more than physiology for the type bounds. Fitted first with the
     importer's "full" labels, the 6 August STAmina session put four of eight tau_washout
     at the full-lung lower bound of 50 s, its 321 s hold's paco2_0 at the 20 mmHg floor
     and gamma at 1.78, and lost 0.15 R2a when the globals were frozen. Relabelled RV, as
     Thomas confirmed, the same traces fit with tau_washout 44 to 46 s interior (the other
     RV sessions: 42 to 77), paco2_0 39 to 43, gamma 1.66, and lose 0.003 when frozen.
     The bounds were doing the diagnosis the label should have.

  8. v7 does not apply below about 120 s of hold or above about 90 % nadir, as its spec
     says: the shallow 8 August and 8 September holds fit at R2a 0.29 to 0.35.


5. VERDICT
----------

v7.08 is confirmed as what its own spec feared: a curve fit with diagnostics, not a
model. Across ten sessions and two oximeters every global parameter moves well outside
the spread v7 called "identified", and pinning all four to another session's values
costs nothing on any session with real desaturation, because the per-hold parameters do
the work. The apnea-window fit quality
survives both ways, which is the definition of a model that does not transfer and cannot
be falsified by this data.

The baseline v8 has to beat is therefore not "mean R2a 0.97 in sample", and not even the
free per-session rows in 3.1. It is table 3.2: on held-out sessions with February's globals
carried over and two free parameters per hold, apnea-window RMSE of 1.1 to 2.4 SpO2
points and a nadir SpO2 error of 1 to 4 points (R2a 0.90 to 0.96). A v8 that
earns its place has to match that with no per-hold free parameters, or with per-hold
parameters that are predicted from things Nadir records (lung volume, pack count,
breathe-up, time since the last hold) rather than fitted. The honest metric is held-out
apnea-window RMSE and nadir error, which is why both are tabulated, not R2a.

What survives from v7 for v8: the Severinghaus ODC and alveolar gas set-up, the sensor /
physiology separation as a hypothesis to test inside one model rather than as two fits,
and the habit of writing the success criteria before the fit. What does not: the
per-hold free parameters, the lambda priors, the lag-as-gamma-kernel sensor model, and
any claim about k_co2.

No v8 modelling is started in this experiment, on purpose. Thirty-six usable holds in
nine sessions is enough to start, but two lung volumes, no FRC, no device provenance,
unreliable labels on the STAmina imports and no captured nadir on the max holds mean the
first v8 step is a sensor model for the app-recorded traces and a lung-volume contrast
that exists in the data, not a hierarchical physiology model.
