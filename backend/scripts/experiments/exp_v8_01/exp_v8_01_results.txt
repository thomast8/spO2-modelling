v8.01 Results: what the two oximeters do to a hold, without ground truth
========================================================================

Date: 2026-10-17
Script:  backend/scripts/experiments/exp_v8_01/exp_v8_01_sensor.py
Outputs: backend/scripts/experiments/exp_v8_01/results/tables.txt   (every table in full)
         results/lag_measures.json, results/posthold_fit.json, results/posthold-<device>.png
Depends on the exp_v8_00 loader (nadir_export.py) and its QC and label override.

Question: the v8 handoff asks for a sensor model per device. v8.00 found that v7's "sensor
lag" absorbed whatever the latent template could not make, and that the PM100 traces looked
different from the Masimo ones around the nadir. This experiment asks what, exactly, differs
between the two oximeters in these recordings, and what of a sensor model is identifiable
when no trace has an arterial reference.

Devices: Masimo MightySat (finger) via STAmina on 21 February and 6 and 8 August 2026;
Medisana PM100 (finger) via Nadir from 2 September 2026 on (Thomas, 17 October). One device
per period, so device, month, finger and training phase are confounded throughout.

What Nadir and BluetoothEngine keep of a PM100 reading, from the code: BluetoothEngine's
PLXSParser decodes the base SpO2 and pulse rate of the PLX Continuous Measurement and skips
the optional SpO2PR-Fast and SpO2PR-Slow fields if the device sends them; it maps the Device
and Sensor Status bits to a contact flag and a quality (`good`, `lowPerfusion`, `searching`,
`noContact`). Nadir's `VitalsRecorder.sample(from:)` keeps SpO2, heart rate, RR intervals and
the contact flag, and drops the quality. Each BLE notification becomes one sample; the
timestamps are the engine's. So a "low perfusion" reading is indistinguishable from a good
one in the export, and whether the PM100 reports an averaged or a fast SpO2 is not knowable
from the recordings.

Inside holds the rate is the same on both devices: 1.0 freeze-jump per 1000 s on the stricter
rule, 3.1 against 3.3 on the looser. The context differs: on the looser rule 13 of the 21
PM100 freezes in holds began mid-descent against 2 of the 9 Masimo ones (2 of 6 against 0 of
3 on the stricter), which is the plateau-then-drop impression v8.00 took from the plots, on
counts too small to lean on. The PM100's jumps are also larger (up to 27 points inside a
hold, 55 outside, against 6 and 10). Its freezes in activity wear (sleep and daytime) run at
four times its own rate in holds and go with contact loss; the Masimo never recorded
activity, so there is no counterpart. The plateau-and-step look of the RV tables in v8.00's
plots is integer oximetry of a slow signal on both devices, not a PM100 trait; v8.00's
write-up was corrected to say so before this file was written.


2. FREEZE MASK (table B)
------------------------

A later fit should treat a frozen run as missing rather than as a plateau in SaO2. With the
stricter rule (a run of 8 s or more followed by a step of 3 or more; the run is masked):

  masimo    14 QC-passing holds,  2954 s of trace,  34 s masked (1.2 %), 4 holds touched
  pm100     26 QC-passing holds,  5264 s of trace,  61 s masked (1.2 %), 5 holds touched

`freeze_mask(t, spo2)` in the script is the rule. It removes about one second in eighty on
either device, so it changes no conclusion in v8.00; it is there so the next fit does not
have to argue about plateaus.


3. MODEL-FREE LAG MEASURES (table C)
------------------------------------

For every QC-passing hold with at least 15 s of recording after it (timestamps, not sample
counts): nadir delay (seconds from the end of the hold to the lowest reading) and onset
(seconds from the end of the hold until the reading has risen two points above its lowest,
looking only after the last time that lowest value is reached, so a bounce before a deeper
nadir is not an onset). Medians with 95 % bootstrap intervals that resample whole recording
sessions, not holds; n is holds, s is sessions, and no interval is given below three sessions:

  group                   n  nadir delay (s)         onset (s)
  masimo, all            13  23 [20, 30]  s=3       29 [27, 38]  n=13 s=3
  pm100, all             19  27 [16, 39]  s=6       37 [35, 47]  n=16 s=6
  masimo, FL              3  29 [n/a]     s=2       37 [n/a]     n=3  s=2
  pm100, FL               6  21 [10, 29]  s=4       36 [27, 39]  n=4  s=4
  masimo, RV              8  20 [n/a]     s=2       26 [n/a]     n=8  s=2
  pm100, RV              13  33 [26, 40]  s=3       38 [36, 48]  n=12 s=3
  masimo, RV, 90-140 s    6  20 [n/a]     s=2       27 [n/a]     n=6  s=2
  pm100, RV, 90-140 s    10  32 [26, 40]  s=3       38 [36, 48]  n=10 s=3

Three PM100 holds have no onset because the trace ends first (4 September RV3 at 43 s of
recording, 4 September FL5 and 5 September FL2 at 20 s); they count in the nadir-delay medians
and not in the onset medians, so the PM100 onset medians are biased short.

The device comparison does not survive the change to session-level resampling as an
inferential result. RV is the one lung volume both devices recorded in numbers, and within it
the Masimo holds come from two sessions (seven on 6 August, one on 21 February) and the PM100
holds from three (4 September, 12 and 16 October). The point estimates still differ in the
same direction: onset of the recovery rise about eleven seconds later on the PM100 (38 against
26 s) and nadir delay thirteen seconds later (32 to 33 against 20 s). But the Masimo RV
interval cannot be estimated from two sessions, the PM100 one rests on three, and over all
holds the intervals overlap (onset 29 [27, 38] against 37 [35, 47]; nadir delay 23 [20, 30]
against 27 [16, 39]). The earlier statement that the intervals do not overlap came from
resampling holds, which treats the seven holds of one afternoon as seven independent
observations; it should not be repeated. What these recordings support is that the PM100 sessions
sit later than the Masimo sessions in the sessions available, not that the devices differ.
The PM100's nadir delay also spreads far wider (1 to 47 s against 10 to 35 s); the widest
values are one session (12 October, 39 to 47 s).

Against the first version of this file the hold counts also changed (PM100 24 to 19, 18 to 13
within RV), because the merged loader no longer keeps the 2 October holds that were attached
to the wrong session or second. That, not the onset or coverage fixes, moved the PM100 medians
(nadir delay 24 to 27, RV 26 to 33); the onset fix removed one onset (4 September RV3, whose
earlier 21 s was a bounce before its nadir at 33 s).

Nadir delay against the slope over the last 30 s of the hold: a pure delay gives the same
nadir delay whatever the slope, a low-pass does not. Pearson r is +0.08 on the Masimo
(13 holds) and -0.33 on the PM100 (19 holds), but leaving one session out moves the PM100
value between -0.63 and +0.01, and it is +0.01 without 12 October. One session carries it,
so nothing is claimed about kernel form.

Heart rate as a covariate: onset = 30 s + 9 s if PM100 + 0 s per %/s of end slope, residual
sd 8 s, and the residual's correlation with end-of-hold heart rate is r = -0.09 over 29
holds. Reported, not modelled: with 13 and 16 holds and the sign of simpler correlations
flipping between devices, there is no HR-dependent lag to put in a model.


4. POST-HOLD SENSOR FIT (table D)
---------------------------------

One small parametric fit per device on the holds with at least 45 s of recording after the
end (Masimo 4 holds in 2 sessions, PM100 8 in 3), window the last 30 s of the hold plus the
recovery. Latent: the end-of-hold descent continues for T seconds, sits on a floor for P
seconds, then rises exponentially to the pre-hold baseline with a per-hold time constant
tau_r. Sensor: a first-order low-pass with time constant tau_s. T, P and tau_s shared per
device, bootstrapped over whole sessions (200 resamples); tau_s profiled on a grid.

  device   n  s  T (s)              P (s)              tau_s (s)         tau_r median  RMSE
  masimo   4  2  13.8 [n/a]         23.6 [n/a]         2.7 [n/a]          7.7 s       4.56
  pm100    8  3  17.3 [11.3, 19.1]  20.6 [19.2, 27.6]  3.9 [2.6, 4.2]    16.6 s       6.02

  The Masimo fit has two sessions, so no interval is reported. The PM100 intervals come from
  resampling three sessions, which allows only a handful of distinct resamples; they are
  narrower than the earlier hold-level ones (T 8.9 to 38.4 s) because of that, not because the
  parameters are better known, and should not be read as precision.

  tau_s profile, RMSE at fixed tau_s:
  masimo   1s 4.64   2s 4.59   4s 4.63   8s 5.45   12s 6.54   16s 7.61   24s 9.59   32s 11.15
  pm100    1s 6.10   2s 6.06   4s 6.02   8s 6.18   12s 6.49   16s 7.12   24s 8.57   32s  9.86

What this fit suggests: within this latent form the sensor's own smoothing is short. The
profile is flat below 4 s and the fit is worse at every tau_s above 8 s on both devices. A
profile under a model that does not fit is not a bound, so this is suggested, not
established. What it cannot say: T and P separately. Resampling
holds, they spanned 9 to 38 s and 0 to 35 s and moved against each other, and at session level
they cannot be bounded at all; only their sum, the onset of the rise (about 38 s on both
devices), is pinned, and section 3 measures that directly. The
first version of this fit, without the floor, drew a V through every recovery and scored
RMSE 5.4; adding the floor helped the Masimo holds and not the PM100 ones. The plots in
results/posthold-*.png show why: holds differ in when the fall stops and when the rise
begins by 20 to 30 s, and one shared T and P per device cannot follow that. An RMSE of 4.6
to 6.0 points on a signal quantised to 1 is a model that does not fit, and it is reported
as one.


5. WHAT WAS AND WAS NOT IDENTIFIABLE
------------------------------------

Identifiable from these recordings:

  - That the plateau-and-step pattern inside holds is the same on both devices, and that
    the PM100's extra freezes are in activity wear and go with contact loss (section 1).
  - A masking rule for frozen runs and its cost, about 1.2 % of hold-seconds (section 2).

Suggested, not established:

  - A later recovery onset on the PM100 within RV, about eleven seconds at matched hold
    length (38 against 26 s). With two Masimo and three PM100 sessions in RV the bootstrap
    over sessions gives no Masimo interval and overlapping ones overall, so this is a
    direction in the point estimates, not a difference. Device, month, finger and training
    phase are confounded in any case (section 3).
  - That the sensor's own low-pass is short, under 8 s, on both devices: a profile under a
    latent form that does not fit (section 4).

Not identifiable:

  - The split of the post-hold delay into lung-to-finger transit and device delay, or into
    transit and plateau (T against P). Only their sum is pinned.
  - Kernel form (pure delay against low-pass) from the slope dependence: one session
    carries the correlation.
  - Any dependence of lag on heart rate.
  - Whether a PM100 freeze is the device, the finger, the diver's movement or the app's
    sampling: the export has no quality flag and no raw frames.
  - Anything about the PM100's averaging mode: the parser skips the fast/slow fields and
    Nadir drops the quality.

So there is no per-device sensor model to carry into v8 yet. What there is: a cleaning rule,
a possible device offset of about ten seconds in recovery onset, to be treated as a nuisance
parameter per recording period until sessions with both oximeters exist, and a suggestion that the smoothing is short. A model of the hold itself that uses these
should treat the sensor as "delay about 25 to 40 s, smoothing under 8 s, integer output,
occasional freezes masked" and fit the delay per hold, which is what v7's delta_h was doing
by another name.


6. WHAT WOULD MAKE IT IDENTIFIABLE
----------------------------------

  1. One session with both oximeters on at once, on two fingers of the same hand, an RV
     table of five or six holds and one full-lung hold, with the cooldown on so every nadir
     is in the trace. Nadir's `VitalsFusion` records one oximeter's SpO2 at a time (the
     first in the diver's order with a fresh reading), so the second device has to be logged
     by STAmina on the Masimo while Nadir records the PM100, with the clocks aligned at the
     first hold. Device against finger then separates within a single hold.
  2. A one-minute raw capture from the PM100 through the signed BluetoothEngine CLI
     (`sensor raw --name PM100 --csv`) with a finger in it, to read the PLX flags byte: if
     the Fast/Slow bits are set, the averaging mode is known and Nadir is recording whichever
     field the parser takes first. This needs Thomas's finger in the device; it is a request,
     not something this session can do.
  3. In Nadir, keep the PLX quality on the sample (BluetoothEngine already derives it) and
     the oximeter model on the session (#439). The quality flag is what would let a freeze be
     attributed; the model is what would let two periods be compared at all.

Nothing here changes the framing rule: all of this is about reading recordings that already
happened. It does not forecast and it does not warn.
